Our story

Fax machines shouldn't be the backbone of modern healthcare.

PulseLink started with a simple, frustrating observation: hospitals somehow built entire buildings full of expertise and equipment that still can't talk to the clinic next door.

Every patient in Kenya has a health record — it's just scattered across every hospital, clinic, lab and pharmacy they've ever visited. A lab result here. A prescription there. An X-ray nobody can find when it actually matters.

We didn't think the answer was to make every provider switch to the same software. That's slow, expensive, and it's been tried. Instead, we built a secure layer that sits between systems that already exist — the way a payment network connects banks without becoming a bank.

The result is a single record a patient can actually own, made of information that already exists — just finally able to move to wherever it's needed, with the patient's say-so at every step.

"The patient owns and controls access to their medical information, while healthcare providers can securely access the data they need to provide better care."
— The idea PulseLink is built around

What we hold onto

Four commitments that don't change as we grow.

Consent isn't a checkbox

Access is granted deliberately, scoped, and can be taken back. Nothing moves without the patient's decision.

We're plumbing, not a destination

We don't ask hospitals to replace what already works. We connect it. Interoperability beats reinvention.

Trust has to be provable

Every access is logged. Every record change is verifiable. Not because we say so — because it's checkable.

Built for where care actually happens

Designed around Kenyan healthcare infrastructure first — with the intent to grow across the region, not away from it.

Speed saves lives

A record that takes three days to arrive is a record that arrives too late. We build for seconds, not business days.

Invisible when it's working

Our best day is one where nobody thinks about PulseLink — they just have the information they need, right when they need it.

Who's building this

A small team of clinicians, engineers, and people who've spent too many hours waiting on records that should already have arrived.

Where we fit in Kenya's health-data landscape

We're not the first to try to solve this, and we don't think we should be the only ones working on it.

Government health information exchange efforts

The Ministry of Health and SHA (Social Health Authority) are building national digital health infrastructure. PulseLink is designed to plug into that ecosystem — not compete with it — the same way we connect to hospital systems rather than replace them.

Other health-tech platforms

Kenya has real momentum in this space already. Our focus is specifically the exchange layer — the plumbing between institutions — rather than being a patient-facing app, hospital EMR, or insurance platform ourselves.

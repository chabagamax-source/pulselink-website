The core idea

The patient holds the key

Every authorised provider connects through one secure layer — instead of every hospital keeping an isolated, disconnected record.

What we do

PulseLink is a health information exchange: a secure layer that lets hospitals, clinics, laboratories, pharmacies and insurers share the medical information they're authorised to see — while the patient controls access.

We're not replacing the software your hospital already runs. We're the connective layer that lets it talk to everyone else's — the same way payment networks quietly connect banks without becoming a bank themselves.

Patients decide who sees their record, when they see it, and how long that access lasts. Nothing is shared by default.

What it actually looks like

One app. Your whole record. A toggle for who sees it.

Open the app, see everything in one place, and grant a hospital or lab temporary access with one tap — down to a specific record, for a specific window of time.

Built to work on an entry-level smartphone and a slow connection — most of Kenya's mobile users are, so we are too.

What we build on

Four principles that don't get compromised, however fast we grow.

Privacy first

Records stay encrypted. Patients choose who can see them, and can revoke access at any time.

Interoperability

Hospitals run different software. PulseLink bridges those systems instead of replacing them.

Speed

A doctor shouldn't wait days for records. Authorised information is available in seconds.

Security

Encrypted databases, role-based permissions, and an audit log on every single action.

How we prove nothing's been quietly changed

Your medical record itself never touches a blockchain. Only a fingerprint of it does.

01

Record is encrypted

Your medical record is encrypted and stored securely.

02

A fingerprint is generated

A one-way cryptographic hash is created from the record.

03

Fingerprint goes on-chain

Only that fingerprint — never the record — is written to the blockchain.

04

Tampering shows immediately

Alter the record and its fingerprint changes — proof, without exposing the data.

Every user type — patient, doctor, nurse, lab staff, pharmacist, administrator — has different permissions. Every action is logged. Every permission is controlled. Every access can be audited.

On sensitive records: HIV status, mental health, and reproductive health records follow the exact same consent model as everything else — never surfaced to any provider without your explicit, specific approval, granted one request at a time. Nothing is shared by category or by default, and consent for one provider never implies consent for another.
